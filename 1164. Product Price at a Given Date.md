# 1164. Product Price at a Given Date
LeetCode: https://leetcode.com/problems/product-price-at-a-given-date/ · Medium

## Problem
Every product starts at a price of 10. The `Products` table logs each price change with the date it took effect. Find every product's price as of `2019-08-16`.

**Products**
| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| product_id | int | part of primary key |
| new_price | int | |
| change_date | date | part of primary key |

**Example**
```
Products:
1 | 20 | 2019-08-14
2 | 50 | 2019-08-14
1 | 30 | 2019-08-15
1 | 35 | 2019-08-16
2 | 65 | 2019-08-17
3 | 20 | 2019-08-18

Output: (1, 35), (2, 50), (3, 10)
```
Product 1's latest change on or before the target date is 35. Product 2's 65 change happens after the target date, so its price is still 50. Product 3's only change is after the target date, so it never left the default price of 10.

## Solution
```sql
SELECT p.product_id,
       p.new_price AS price
FROM Products p
JOIN (
    SELECT product_id,
           MAX(change_date) AS latest_date
    FROM Products
    WHERE change_date <= '2019-08-16'
    GROUP BY product_id
) latest
    ON p.product_id = latest.product_id
   AND p.change_date = latest.latest_date

UNION

SELECT product_id,
       10 AS price
FROM Products
GROUP BY product_id
HAVING MIN(change_date) > '2019-08-16';
```

1. The subquery finds each product's *latest* change date on or before the target date, not its highest price; `MAX(new_price)` would give the wrong answer whenever an earlier change happened to set a higher price than a later one (product 1 in the example: the highest price ever set is 35, but that's also its latest one, so it's easy to miss this distinction on a case where they'd diverge).
2. The join back to `Products` needs both `product_id` and `change_date` in the `ON` clause. Matching on `product_id` alone would pull in every historical row for that product; the `change_date` match pins it down to exactly the one row for the latest date.
3. `HAVING MIN(change_date) > '2019-08-16'` catches products, like product 3, whose very first price change happens after the target date, meaning they never had a chance to move off the default price. `HAVING` (not `WHERE`) is required because the condition is evaluated on the aggregated `MIN(change_date)` per product.
4. `UNION` merges the two cases (products with an applicable price change, and products still at their default) into one result, and de-duplicates automatically, which is safe here since no product can land in both branches at once.
